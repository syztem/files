The manifest

Most software flashes at you. We would rather exhale beside you, letting light keep time with you.

Aether treats motion as a material with weight. Every curve is tuned against the panel it lands on, so an interface on a 60 hertz laptop feels identical to one on a 120 hertz display.

Colour is never picked, it is measured. The engine samples the ambient light around a device and renders palettes that belong in the room they are being used.

And when a moment needs nothing at all, the surface goes quiet. Restraint is the feature people notice last and miss most.

Questions

Asked often,
answered plainly.

Yes, completely. The engine ships as a 41 kilobyte local runtime. Nothing is measured remotely, no colour ever leaves the device, and every surface renders identically in airplane mode.

On a five-year-old handset the whole runtime costs roughly 0.4 percent of a full charge over an eight-hour session. Ambient sampling is throttled to two hertz when the surface is idle and stops entirely in quiet mode.

They specify intent instead. Declare that a surface should feel warm, unhurried, or urgent, and the engine chooses the curve, the duration and the falloff. Override any of it with a single line when you want to.

Reduced-motion preferences are honoured automatically and are never a bolt-on. All four palettes clear seven to one contrast on every surface, and every transition can be replaced with a crossfade per component.

You do, entirely, including the palettes the engine samples on your behalf. We keep no telemetry, train no models on your surfaces, and offer a single-click export of the whole system as plain SVG and CSS.
